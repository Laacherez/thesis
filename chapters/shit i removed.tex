This consists in introducing an auxiliary response field $f(x,y)$, defined over one spatial period in $x$ and over the thermally accessible range of the elastic coordinate $y$. The field $f$ solves the linear cell problem
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} f = S,
    \label{Kubo_cellproblem}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{L}$ is the Fokker-Planck operator associated with the equilibrium dynamics and $S$ is the source generated by an infinitesimal forcing along the transport direction. The uniqueness of the solution is fixed by imposing

\begin{equation}
    \int f(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x \,\mathrm{d}y = 0~.
    \label{jubo_boundary}
\end{equation}

Once $f$ is known, the effective diffusivity follows from the Kubo formula

\begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mu_{11} - \frac{ \int f(x,y) \left[ \mu_{11}\partial_x\phi(x) + \frac{\mu_{12}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}y \right] \, \mathrm{d}x \, \mathrm{d}y
}{\int P_{\mathrm{eq}}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}~.
\label{Kubo_final}
\end{equation}
